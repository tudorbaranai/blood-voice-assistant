Blood · screen states

A full-screen window that pops up on "Hey Blood". Each state needs its own animation, in a Berserk or Vagabond manga style. These are ideas to design from, not final decisions.

1 wake→2 greeting→3 listening→4 thinking→5 speaking→6 closing

One conversation in order. Saying "Hey Blood" again jumps back to wake from any state, so every animation must be interruptible.

Rules for every state

  1. Each state has its own clear shape and motion, recognisable from 2–3 m away in half a second. Colour alone is not enough.
  2. Every state has an entrance, a loop and an exit, and can be cut off at any moment.
  3. The voice level drives the motion, smoothed: reacts fast to louder, settles slowly.
  4. Two speeds: drawn elements move at 8–12 frames per second with a slight "line boil"; text and layout move smoothly.
  5. Brush style for titles and effects; a clean, readable font for the transcript (like subtitles in Sekiro or Ghost of Tsushima).
  6. Leave space empty. Vagabond's power is blank paper, Berserk's is black.
  7. Original marks only: no copied manga drawings or symbols. Blood gets its own sigil.

Fixed facts

  • Laptop screen, 1920 × 1080, 144 Hz, full screen, often in a dark room.
  • Viewed from across the room (2–3 m) as often as up close.
  • One local web page (HTML, CSS, JavaScript), no online services.
  • Live updates arrive as small messages: state, heard, answer, level.
  • Tudor's name is always written "Tudor" on screen.
Suggested direction: a Berserk base (black and crimson, easy on the eyes at night, fits the name "Blood") that moves like Vagabond (wet brush strokes, ink bleeding).
